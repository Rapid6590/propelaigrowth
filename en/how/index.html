How we think

Metrics are outcomes. Behavior is what you can work on.

At Propel AI we use behavioral economics, with AI as a working tool, to make the behavior you want the easier choice — and we try to do it before anyone adds budget.

How we look at a problem

When a number is not moving, most companies look at the people and ask why they are not trying harder. We look one step earlier, at the environment those people are working in: what they see, how many steps the task takes, and what happens if they do nothing. That is the step most companies skip, and it is usually where the answer is.

So we don't start with "how do we grow revenue." We start with a narrower question: who, at what moment, is not doing which action?

What does "changing the environment" look like? Three examples

Change the default

A green electricity plan — same plan, same price. When customers had to tick a box to opt in, 7% took it. When they were enrolled by default and could untick to opt out, 69% stayed in.

Offer two options instead of one

On a product page, showing a single option led 9% of visitors to buy. Showing two options side by side raised that to 66%.

Speak first

When a customer asks "roughly how much," opening with "what's your budget?" and opening with a reasoned number lead to very different places. The side that names a number first tends to pull the final price its way.

How this differs from the usual approach

We aim at the environment, not the person

Explaining again, issuing an instruction, repeating the message — these work for as long as someone keeps pushing, and the numbers tend to slide back the moment the effort stops.

We look before we spend

Find the step where things are stuck first, and only then decide whether money is actually needed.

You judge, AI supplements, you decide

What AI offers is a second opinion, and we treat it as a hypothesis until the numbers say otherwise.

This way of looking at a problem follows a fixed path

We call it PROPEL™ — six stations, from pinpointing the behavior to looping back to the numbers: Pinpoint, Reconstruct, Obstacle, Prescribe, Experiment, Loop. The full-day workshop walks that path from beginning to end, and every station leaves the team with one sheet of paper they can use the following week.
