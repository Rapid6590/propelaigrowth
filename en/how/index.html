How we look at a problem

When a metric stalls, it is tempting to ask people to try harder. We first look at what they face when it is time to act: what they see, how many steps the task takes, and what happens if they do nothing. That is the step that usually gets skipped, and it is often where the answer is.

What does "changing the environment" look like? Three examples

Change the default

Same green-power plan, same price. When customers had to opt in, 7% signed up. When they were signed up by default and could opt out, 69% stayed on it.

Show two options, not one

In a published choice experiment, 9% chose to buy when shown one option. With two options side by side, 66% did.

Name a number first

When a customer asks "Roughly how much?", you can answer with "What's your budget?" or with a reasoned number. The side that names a number first tends to pull the final price its way.

How this differs from the usual approach

We aim at the environment, not the person

Explaining again, issuing an instruction, repeating the message — these work for as long as someone keeps pushing, and the numbers tend to slide back the moment the effort stops.
