About

We do one thing: make the behavior you want the easier choice

The proposal went out, and the client never took the next step. The AI tools were bought, and the team never really picked them up. The SOP was written clearly, and frontline teams kept working the way they always had. These are the situations Propel AI was built for. Through courses and workshops, we teach teams to use behavioral economics and AI to find out why customers don't move forward and colleagues don't act, and then to adjust the process, the message, and the environment in which those decisions are made.

Three things we believe

An outcome is not a behavior

Revenue and adoption are results. What a team can actually work on is who does which action, and when.

Personality is not the cause

"He's lazy" or "she doesn't care" is where the conversation usually ends, and it is rarely where the problem is. More often the step takes too much effort, is too easy to forget, or nobody has gone first.

A hypothesis is not a conclusion

Every fix is a guess until it has been tried on a small scale and the numbers have been read. Then the team keeps it, adjusts it, or replaces it.

Why behavioral economics

Companies know their own sales process very well. What they see less clearly is the process from the customer's side: what they have to do at each step, what they are worried about, and who else has to sign off before they can say yes. Those things the customer must get done don't always appear anywhere in our process, and they are exactly what behavioral economics is about. Beyond need and willingness, how the options are laid out, how many steps it takes, and how the reminder arrives all shape a decision.

For a long time, behavioral economics was something governments and large corporations brought in through consultants. Most companies have heard of it and would like to try it on their own business, but they rarely know where to begin: the research takes time to read, and there have been few practical tools that connect the findings to the steps of everyday work. That connection is what we have spent the last few years building — turning research findings into steps a business team can practice at work, and then watching the effect in their own numbers.

What teams practice in the room

1

Break it into a visible action

"Grow revenue" or "get people to use the tool" becomes who, at what moment, does which action.

2

Find where it sticks and design a fix

Work out why that action might stall, and design a change that doesn't begin by adding budget.

3

Read your own numbers

Try the change on a small scale, look at the numbers, and then decide whether to keep it, adjust it, or move to another spot. How long to watch depends on how often the behavior happens; for frequent behaviors, one to two weeks is enough for a first look.

Along the way the team works with an AI tool we built ourselves, backed by a library of 67 common sticking points and 101 fixes. The order never changes: the team thinks first, AI adds what might have been overlooked, the instructor guides the judgment, and the team decides. After the course, the prompts go with you — one written prompt for each exercise, so that back at the office you can paste your real situation into whatever AI tool you normally use and walk through the same steps again.
