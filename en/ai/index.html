Where AI fits

AI makes things. Things don’t become behavior on their own.

Copy, slide decks, reports, code: AI can produce all of them, and the cost keeps falling. A customer saying yes, a team following the new process, a resident completing an application: none of these happens just because one more document exists.

AI is the navigation, not the accelerator.It can work out the route. For the car to move, someone still has to press the pedal.

Everyone is using AI. Why hasn’t the company felt it?

AI has made “making it” faster. “Getting the other side to act” is no easier.And revenue only moves when they act.

You are not aloneIndividuals got faster. Company numbers have not caught up.

80%of respondents say AI has improved their own productivity
37%say AI has contributed to their company’s earnings

The reasonThe difference is the workplace, not individual willingness

Relative importance for how much AI helps, as people report it

67%Organizational factors: culture, manager support, talent practices
32%Individual mindset and behavior
SoWhat we do is the step after AI has done its part: getting customers to reply, and getting teams to follow through.We do it by changing what a person faces at the moment of decision: what they see, how many steps it takes, and when the reminder arrives.

External surveys, not Propel AI client results. Sources: McKinsey, The state of AI in 2026 (1,719 respondents, 97 countries). Microsoft, 2026 Work Trend Index (20,000 people who use AI at work, 10 markets; 67% and 32% are relative importance in a model, not causation).

Before: one version for everyone. With AI: one for each person.

The same sign, the same letter, the same default: that was most of what could be done before. Now data records what each person actually did, and AI helps decide what to say to them and when. “Change the environment, not the person” has not changed. What has changed is that the reminder can fit each person’s situation.

Two examples already in use

Government

Japan’s Ministry of the Environment: a home energy report that shows how similar homes compare

Five electricity and gas utilities sent it to about 300,000 households over four years. Each household received a report written from its own usage. Energy use fell by about 2% on average.

The Ministry calls this direction BI-Tech: behavioral science combined with data and AI, so that each household gets its own version.

Source: Ministry of the Environment, Japan, nudge program; Oracle Japan and Jyukankyo Research Institute (field trials, fiscal 2017–2020)

Business

Mercedes-Benz ATTENTION ASSIST: it learns how you drive, then suggests a rest

In the first 20 minutes of a trip, the system records how you are driving, using 70 parameters. Later, when the steering shows the corrections typical of a tired driver, a coffee cup lights up on the dashboard and a tone sounds.

There is no “Do not drive tired” sign. It reminds that one driver, at that moment.

Source: Mercedes-Benz press material (introduced in 2009)

Taking it back

AI can write the sentence well. Which person, at which moment, and which part to change: that is your judgment, and it is what the program trains.

Behavior is not decided by the person alone. Much of it is decided by the environment. And the environment is something you can arrange in advance.
  • Do they want to?
  • Is it easy to do?
  • Is there a reminder at the moment it matters?
